    TOY 2n = 6 GENOME

    Parental strands, new complements, and copied loci

    Each row is one toy chromosome. During S phase, the bright copying frontier advances across all six templates. Once copying completes, each sister chromatid holds one retained parental strand and one new complement strand.

    Base letters form a fixed illustrative genome. The model derives complements with A↔T and C↔G; it does not claim to render a natural organism's genome.

    BIOLOGICAL CARRIER

    What the model performs

    G1 contains six unreplicated toy chromosomes. S phase copies every locus once. G2 admits the cell only when the copy is complete and complementarity is restored. Mitosis separates sister chromatids; cytokinesis closes two equally provisioned daughter-cell records.

    Parental template
    Retained molecular sequence available for copying.
    Copy difference
    Uncopied loci or a declared mismatch.
    Transport
    Replication completion and spindle-directed chromatid movement.
    Comparison
    Complementarity, copy-once, and alignment checks.
    Return
    One complete chromosome set in each daughter cell.
    Receipt
    Append-only event history plus deterministic replay.

    SOURCE-BOUND INTERPRETATION

    What remains open

    The reader is a formal teaching construction with native biological rules. Its structural lens describes retained form through division; it does not derive DNA chemistry from the Clear Onyx carrier or substitute its display for empirical measurement.

    The biology anchors are semiconservative replication, once-per-cycle genome copying, checkpoint control, chromatid segregation, and cytokinesis. Meiosis, recombination, fertilization, development, and gene regulation belong in later, separately bounded instruments.

    BIOLOGICAL REFERENCE NOTE
    [1] Meselson, M. & Stahl, F. W. (1958). The Replication of DNA in Escherichia coli. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 44, 671–682. doi:10.1073/pnas.44.7.671. The model's one-parental/one-new-strand teaching rule is bounded by this semiconservative result.

[2] National Human Genome Research Institute. DNA Replication; Cell Cycle; Mitosis. These reference pages supply the ordinary cell-cycle distinction used here: S phase copies DNA, and mitosis distributes a copy of each chromosome to each daughter cell.
