# I. The living machine must maintain its working parts

The genome specifies proteins.

Metabolism supplies work.

Neither is sufficient if the proteins and organelles that execute cellular
Function are allowed to accumulate damage without control.

The maintenance layer therefore begins with a simple physical fact:

\[
\boxed{
\text{functional macromolecule}
\to
\text{use}
\to
\text{damage / misfold / turnover}
\to
\text{repair, refold, replace, or remove}.
}
\]

This is the biological transition from hereditary Memory to active
self-maintenance.

# III. Chaperones — assistance before destruction

Molecular chaperones bind non-native or folding proteins and influence their
access to productive folding pathways.

Their office includes:

- preventing inappropriate aggregation;
- stabilizing non-native intermediates;
- assisting folding or refolding;
- coordinating assembly / disassembly;
- triaging damaged proteins toward other quality-control pathways.

The first protein-quality branch is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{non-native protein}
\to
\text{chaperone interaction}
\to
\text{productive folding / refolding}
}
\]

when recovery remains available.

Repair precedes replacement where the molecular carrier still supports it.

---

# IV. Ubiquitin–proteasome system — selective material removal

The ubiquitin–proteasome system supplies regulated protein destruction.

Target proteins can be covalently modified with ubiquitin through E1, E2, and
E3 enzyme systems.

Substrate specificity is strongly shaped by ubiquitin ligases and the context
of the ubiquitin signal.

The 26S proteasome recognizes appropriate degradation signals, unfolds the
substrate in an ATP-dependent process, and translocates it into a proteolytic
chamber.

The material chain is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{protein}
\to
\text{degradation signal}
\to
\text{ubiquitylation}
\to
\text{proteasome engagement}
\to
\text{unfolding}
\to
\text{proteolysis}.
}
\]

Protein quality control therefore contains an actual **remove-and-replace**
operation.

The protein identity may disappear from the active pool while its amino-acid
material is recycled into the larger cellular economy.

---

# V. Autophagy–lysosome system — larger-scale cellular turnover

Autophagic pathways deliver cytoplasmic material to lysosomes for degradation.

At the scale relevant here, autophagy can remove:

- long-lived proteins;
- aggregates;
- portions of cytoplasm;
- damaged organelles.

The proteasome and lysosome therefore occupy different degradation carriers.

A useful minimum split is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{protein-scale selective degradation}
\quad|\quad
\text{larger cargo / organelle degradation}.
}
\]

Proteostasis is distributed across both.

---

# VI. Ribosome-associated quality control — inspection during synthesis

Protein quality control can begin before translation finishes.

Stalled ribosomes can generate incomplete or aberrant nascent chains.

Ribosome-associated quality control recognizes defined stalled translation
states and can target the aberrant nascent protein for degradation.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{translation}
\to
\text{stall / aberrant nascent chain}
\to
\text{quality-control read}
\to
\text{termination / extraction / degradation}.
}
\]

The cell does not need to finish a defective protein before quality control can
act.

This is another instance of continuation being governed by an intermediate
state read.

# VII. The unfolded protein response — proteostasis at organelle scale

The endoplasmic reticulum folds and assembles a large fraction of secreted and
membrane proteins.

When ER protein-folding demand exceeds available capacity, unfolded-protein
response signalling changes cellular Function.

Its three canonical metazoan signalling arms are associated with:

\[
\boxed{
IRE1,\qquad PERK,\qquad ATF6.
}
\]

The UPR can:

- reduce incoming protein-folding load;
- alter translation;
- increase folding / chaperone capacity;
- increase degradation capacity;
- expand secretory-pathway machinery;
- reprogram transcription and metabolism.

The control loop is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{ER folding state}
\to
\text{stress sensor}
\to
\text{signalling}
\to
\text{changed protein-production / repair capacity}
\to
\text{new ER state}.
}
\]

This is homeostatic control inside the protein-production system.

# VIII. Proteostasis has a viability boundary

The 2025 UPR review makes the control logic especially clear: UPR signalling is
rheostatic and context-dependent.

Moderate stress can produce adaptive restoration.

Persistent or severe unresolved stress can change the signalling outcome toward
programmed cell death.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{protein damage}
\to
\text{adaptation / repair}
\to
\text{restored function}
}
\]

different cell-fate branch.

The control criterion is viability at the cell / organism scale.

# XI. Fusion and fission are different maintenance operations

Mitochondrial fusion joins mitochondrial compartments and can mix components
across the mitochondrial network.

Mitochondrial fission divides a mitochondrial carrier into separate daughters.

The two operations therefore act differently:

\[
\boxed{

\[

Their balance contributes to mitochondrial distribution, content exchange,
adaptation to metabolic demand, inheritance, and quality control.

One process does not invert the other in a simple one-step algebra.

They participate in a larger dynamic network.
