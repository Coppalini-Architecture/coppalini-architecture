# I. Sensation starts at transduction

A sensory receptor couples a physical or chemical variable to a change in cellular
state.

Write

\[
\boxed{
T_s:
S_{\mathrm{phys}}
\to
X_{\mathrm{receptor}}.
}
\]

Examples include:

- photon absorption;
- stereociliary deflection;
- membrane stretch;
- temperature-sensitive channel activation;
- ligand binding;
- tissue-damage chemistry.

The receptor output is already a transformed representation.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\textbf{stimulus}
\neq
\textbf{receptor state}.
}
\]

Sensation begins at this lawful coupling.

---

# II. Receptor response is selective

Every sensory receptor has a response function over a restricted domain.

A generic receptor read is

\[
\boxed{
r_i=s_i(\xi),
}
\]

where \(\xi\) is an environmental or bodily variable.

Different receptors can respond differently to the same stimulus.

Different stimuli can sometimes generate overlapping receptor responses.

The nervous system therefore receives a population code rather than an
unmediated copy of the world.

---

# III. Vision — photon to photoreceptor

Rods and cones form the principal image-forming photoreceptor populations of the
vertebrate retina.

A photon absorbed by a visual pigment initiates the phototransduction cascade:

\[
\boxed{
\text{photon}
\to
\text{activated opsin}
\to
\text{transducin}
\to
PDE
\to
\downarrow cGMP
\to
\text{channel closure}
\to
\text{hyperpolarization}
\to
\downarrow\text{glutamate release}.
}
\]

Thus light produces a graded electrical and synaptic Difference.

The first visual Read is molecular.

---

# IV. Darkness is an active retinal reference

Vertebrate photoreceptors are relatively depolarized in darkness.

Light closes cyclic-nucleotide-gated channels and hyperpolarizes them.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{dark state}
\to
\text{photon Difference}
\to
\text{reduced glutamate output}.
}
\]

The dark condition is a physiological reference state.

It is not absence of retinal activity.

This gives a native physical carrier beneath the project's earlier use of a dark
control background.

---

# V. Rods and cones have different read regimes

Rods are specialized for dim-light sensitivity.

Individual rods can respond to single absorbed photons.

Cones operate at higher light levels with faster temporal response and support
high-acuity and color vision.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{same light field}
\to
\text{different receptor families}
\to
\text{different useful reads}.
}
\]

The visual system has no single universal photon Reader.

---

# VI. Retina is already a neural computation layer

The retina contains:

- photoreceptors;
- horizontal cells;
- bipolar cells;
- amacrine cells;
- ganglion cells.

These circuits extract and transform information about:

- contrast;
- increments/decrements;
- time;
- color;
- motion;
- spatial organization.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\textbf{the optic nerve carries retinal computation,
not a pixel-perfect copy of incident light.}
}
\]

The retina is part of the Reader.

# VII. ON and OFF — real inverse-oriented retinal channels

Cone pathways split into ON and OFF channels.

The ON pathway signals increments relative to background.

The OFF pathway signals decrements relative to background.

Schematically:

\[
\boxed{
\Delta L>0
\to
\mathsf{ON},
\qquad
\Delta L<0
\to
\mathsf{OFF}.
}
\]

The photoreceptor-to-ON-bipolar synapse includes a sign inversion.

The corresponding OFF pathway is sign-preserving at that junction.

This is a real biological inverse-oriented read.

It is not a global left/right handedness claim.

---

# VIII. Optical inversion and synaptic inversion are different

The eye contains at least two distinct inversions.

## Optical

The retinal image is geometrically inverted relative to the external visual
field.

## Synaptic

ON bipolar signalling reverses the sign of the photoreceptor glutamate relation.

These are different carriers and operations.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{optical inversion}
\neq
\text{retinal synaptic inversion}.
}
\]

This is an important mirror firewall.

# XI. Hearing — vibration to receptor current

The cochlea transforms sound-driven mechanical motion into neural signals.

Sound causes mechanical motion of cochlear structures.

\text{synaptic transmission}
\to
\text{auditory nerve}.
}

Hair cells are literal mechanical-to-electrical transducers.

# XII. Auditory timing is exceptionally fast

Auditory hair cells can respond on very short time scales.

- communication.

The auditory system therefore provides a native biological carrier for
high-temporal-resolution Difference.

It does not imply that all biological timing is auditory.

# XV. Vestibular system — inertial Read

The vestibular apparatus measures motion and orientation of the head.

The semicircular canals primarily detect angular acceleration.

The otolith organs detect linear acceleration and gravitationally related head

\[

\text{rotational acceleration}

\text{utricle/saccule}

\text{linear acceleration / gravity-related orientation}
\end{array}
}
\]

The vestibular system is a biological inertial guidance system.

\[
\boxed{
\text{stimulus}
\to
\text{transduction}
\to
\text{neural representation}
\to
\text{distributed perceptual availability}.
}
\]

A report, choice, or action can then provide a downstream receipt.

What remains for the next pass is the explicit relationship among:

- perceptual availability;
- attention;
- working memory;
- conscious report;
- language;
- subjective experience.

The present pass does not collapse those terms.

NCBI neuroscience:

## Active sensing / corollary discharge

Active-sensing literature owns the perception–action sampling loop.

Corollary-discharge/efference-copy literature owns motor-to-sensory prediction and
self-generated sensory compensation.

Cerebellar / VOR literature owns predictive motor calibration and sensory-error
adaptation.

transfer, and network-level changes in sensory decision performance.
