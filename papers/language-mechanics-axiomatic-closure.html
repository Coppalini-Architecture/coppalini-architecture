1. Human entrance

A human being begins in dependence. Before a person can speak, choose, record, compare, or explain, another living body and a surrounding field of care carry that person through time: nourishment, temperature, sleep, protection, touch, rhythm, and response. The first continuity of a life is therefore a maintained relation in motion. [1]

That relation widens through family, friendship, food, shelter, work, schools, books, streets, tools, customs, and institutions. Each supplies a way to meet a later occurrence with a usable prior coordinate. Language belongs among these carriers. A word can orient attention, request a comparison, preserve a procedure, and bring a result back into common use. [2]

The carrier itself continues to move. Morning returns as a recognizable configuration while yesterday remains at a distinct address. A familiar face can return in readable form while both people have crossed further occurrences. Memory makes prior comparison available; it does not flatten the interval through which the carriage advanced.

The question is therefore exact: under what conditions can an advancing relation return in readable form, be recognized at a stated resolution, and remain available for use without erasing the difference carried by time?

2. Declared setting

The first coordinate is the occurrence address. It keeps an advancing event available for comparison even when a later reader output matches an earlier one. A reader at declared resolution ρ is a map

Define reader equivalence on occurrences by

os ≡ρ ot  ⟺  rρ(c(s)) = rρ(c(t)).

For s < t, a return at resolution ρ is the conjunction os ≡ρ ot. The quotient groups occurrences by what this reader can read as the same form. It is a coarse-grained reader result: the occurrence addresses remain distinct coordinates inside the comparison.

  2. Recurring procedure. A return claim applies the same declared reader relation rρ to both addresses. The procedure may include calibration and uncertainty records as part of ρ.

4. Closure theorem

THEOREM / RETURN WITH ADVANCE

Reader equality and occurrence difference cohere exactly.

For s, t ∈ T with s < t, if os ≡ρ ot, then both of the following hold:

rρ(c(s)) = rρ(c(t))   and   os ≠ ot.

Proof. Reader equivalence gives the first equality by definition. Since s < t, we have s ≠ t. Equality of ordered pairs requires equality of their first coordinates, so (s,c(s)) ≠ (t,c(t)). Therefore a returned read and an advancing occurrence are jointly typed facts. □

This is the minimum closure required for ordinary recognition. It lets a comparison say precisely what has returned—equality under a declared reader—while keeping the carried advance visible as a difference of addresses. A recurrent phase, a remembered word, a repeated color coordinate, or a familiar rhythm can be usable again as an addressable recurrence through a continuing carriage.

Arithmetic closure

Classical arithmetic supplies a separate, exact case. Let 0 be a natural number, S its successor operation, 1 = S(0), and 2 = S(S(0)). With recursive addition

n + 0 = n,    n + S(m) = S(n + m),

the derivation closes:

1 + 1 = S(0) + S(0) = S(S(0) + 0) = S(S(0)) = 2.
